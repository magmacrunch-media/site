about

magmacrunch media, since 2023

"sometimes it's quite melodious; other times it's noise"

Magmacrunch media is a creative collective that makes music, film, video games, web tools, writing, and more, from Pennsylvania, California, and New Hampshire.

McCoy brothers Jake, James, and Jon grew up playing music at Frogwood Manor in a forested neighborhood of New Hampshire, and C.P. Rutledge grew up playing music at Spirit Castle, across the street. With other musicians living on the same block, this micro-scene was home to a host of bands and projects over the years, including Vinny Bobarino, Juanito Thompson, The Four B's, and Bottle Boys, along with solo material by Brother Dynamo and Jon/Woah. School and work later took Jake and Jon to Iowa City, where they played in Texas Hold'Em Lava Dome with Stephen McMillan and recorded at The Tuna Can.

By 2023 all of this music needed a home, and there was more on the way. So Jake in Pennsylvania, C.P. in New Hampshire, and Jon in California founded magmacrunch media that year, as an independent record label for a back catalog of home recordings.

In 2024 the label began distributing music, new and old, to streaming platforms and online stores, ranging from post-rock to indie folk to riot-grrrl punk. Magmacrunch music followed in 2025 as a publishing entity. Newer acts have since joined the long-time members in the catalog, among them Dag Henderson and, most recently, Sex Van Floor Plan of Pennsylvania. Stephen McMillan, who went on from Texas Hold'Em Lava Dome to form Audio Sound Paper, has started collaborating too, as a developer as well as on music and field recordings. In August 2026, magmacrunch media became a limited liability company (LLC) in Pennsylvania.

Building this website is what took the collective past music. The visual section holds over a dozen no-budget music videos, along with photography and collage. The arcade holds a couple of dozen browser games, most of them built on our own engines rather than anything off the shelf, and some have been ported back onto real Nintendo Wii hardware and to iOS. The ware section has creative utilities anyone can use in a browser, a programming mini-language, and a few tools for developers. The archive keeps the history above, and press, still in development, will collect interdisciplinary writing.

Traditional record labels never appealed to us. Giving up music rights and ending up in debt to a label, after working so hard just to get there, felt wrong. So we release our own music, and make the music we want to make, whether or not it is what people expect to hear. There are no contracts and no advances to pay back. The collective works with a rotating cast of producers and musicians, all of them friends or family, on home recording and production, and everyone keeps ownership of their recordings and songwriting. More broadly, we want to help people put out records they could not afford to make in a paid studio.

We also like old technology, which is why some releases come out on physical or obscure formats, and why so many of the games look like they belong to an earlier decade. Next, the collective is branching further into art and technology, with plans for building hardware, making documentaries, and more.
