    How to use PUNCH//ROLL

    Write notes

    Each row of the roll is a pitch and each column a step of the grid. Click an empty square to add a note, drag it to move it, and drag its right edge to make it longer. Right-click a note to delete it. Shift-drag across empty grid to select several notes at once.

    Pick a track

    The panel on the left holds the tracks: two pulse channels, a triangle and noise; Add track makes up to six. Click one, or press 1 to 6, and new notes go onto it. M mutes the current track and S solos it. Click the keyboard beside the roll to hear a pitch on that track.

    Make a beat

    Steps, at the top of the panel, swaps the roll for a step sequencer: a row for each track and a column for each step of the grid. Set some tracks to the drums (Kick, Snare, the hi-hats), then click squares to add hits and click them again to take them away; drag along a row to paint several. It is the same song, so the hits are notes in the roll too. G switches between the two. Tick Drums only, in the grid's corner, to see just the drum tracks.

    Swing, at the top of the track panel under Tuning, makes every second sixteenth play a little late, for a shuffle; at 100% it is a full triplet swing. The notes stay where you put them, and only the playing changes.

    Play it

    Press Space or Play. The play button pauses (Shift+Space), and playing again goes on from there; the square stops and goes back to where playback starts, and the button before them takes that back to the start. Click the ruler along the top to choose where playback starts, and untick Loop to play through once. BPM, Bars and Grid in the toolbar set the speed, the length and how finely notes snap. Double-click the ruler to add a tempo change.

    Shape the sound

    Click or drag in the VEL lane under the roll to make notes louder or softer. The meter menu at the top left sets the time signature, and the Tuning menu swaps the usual twelve semitones for 19, 24 (quarter tones) or 31 equal steps to the octave. Each note can also be detuned by a few cents.

    Each track shows its sound: one cycle of the wave and its harmonics (a drum shows one hit), and ENV draws the envelope as a curve. Hover a key to read its pitch in hertz.

    Keep it

    Save writes a .punch song file, and Open loads one back; on the desktop, double-clicking a .punch file opens it too. Export renders the song to an audio file: WAV or FLAC, which keep every sample, or MP3 or Ogg Vorbis, which are far smaller. Ctrl+Z undoes almost anything.

    Every key is listed under Help, Keyboard shortcuts (or press F1).
